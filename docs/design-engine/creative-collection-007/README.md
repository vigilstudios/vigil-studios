### P01 · The merchant’s edit

**Purpose and structure:** One editorial lead, a written buying perspective, and a smaller supporting index. Merchandising allocates attention instead of giving every product equal weight.

**Contract:** editorialNote + 3–5 {product, note}; each product carries identity, price, optional sale comparison, category, availability, media and destination.

**Media:** One product image each; landscape and portrait remain contained. The lead may be model photography; no hard crop required.

**Responsive:** Desktop preserves the mechanism described above. Tablet narrows wells/rails and simplifies supporting shelves; mobile: Lead image and product stay paired; supporting products become a compact two-column shelf with full-width editorial note between them.

**Current interaction:** Product links open focus-managed local destination previews. A future host supplies routes.

**Motion proposal:** Propose StaggerReveal for supporting items; lead visible at rest. None implemented. Current mode is none; no autoplay, automatic progression or smooth programmatic scrolling.

**Compatibility:** Inset document flow; quiet navigation and Front Page Hero, Open Letter before, compact CTA after. Avoid a second asymmetric image index immediately adjacent.

**Three-context result:** Streetwear uses three layers and a lead model plate; skincare uses three ritual items with GBP sale pricing; audio grows to five items, preserving the lead/support distinction at higher density.

**Typography / art:** AFTER HOURS editorial + publication; morrow playful + salon; FORM / FREQUENCY neo-grotesk + precision. Five existing semantic type roles retain profile weight, tracking, case and measure; the mechanism sets local hierarchy.

**Rejected first answer:** Equal-size product cards with a magazine heading.

**Limits:** Curated attention cannot substitute for exhaustive catalog browsing. Five items maximum.

### P02 · Catalog ledger

**Purpose and structure:** A searchable-looking but explicitly bounded catalog register prioritizes category, availability and price in aligned scan lines. A native filter disclosure leaves the product index dominant.

**Contract:** products[5–24] + totalCatalogLabel; local category/availability filter and price order work only on this fixture slice.

**Media:** Small optional product thumbnails. Image absence preserves row identity and measurements.

**Responsive:** Desktop preserves the mechanism described above. Tablet narrows wells/rails and simplifies supporting shelves; mobile: Rows become a two-line register: thumbnail/title then category/status/price. Filters use a native disclosure; no horizontal table drag.

**Current interaction:** Category, available-only and sort controls update fixture rows and announce count. No network search or pagination engine.

**Motion proposal:** None proposed; row positions remain stable until an explicit filter/sort action. Current mode is none; no autoplay, automatic progression or smooth programmatic scrolling.

**Compatibility:** Dense inset document flow; Contents Navigation and Open Circuit Hero. Follow with an open Story or Media section to relieve density.

**Three-context result:** Streetwear uses six records across three categories; skincare uses five across ritual stages; audio uses six records including a media-free digital subscription. Availability and sale labels survive every context. Sorting is a bounded numeric listed-amount demonstration; production needs billing-basis-aware facets.

**Typography / art:** AFTER HOURS technical + precision; morrow neo-grotesk + precision; FORM / FREQUENCY brutalist + billboard. Five existing semantic type roles retain profile weight, tracking, case and measure; the mechanism sets local hierarchy.

**Rejected first answer:** Tiny versions of a standard product card grid.

**Limits:** Displayed total is a demo label, not a live inventory total. Real facets, pagination, URL state and result announcements require a host adapter.

## Backend assumptions and ownership

The intended boundary is **Commerce Data Adapter → Vigil Product Contract → Visual Component**. No adapter is implemented. A future host supplies normalized identity, destination, money, media and availability, and is responsible for pagination, server facets, market/currency, tax-inclusive/exclusive labels, locale, price freshness, stock truth and variant IDs. Displayed availability is a supplied label, never a live inventory query. No displayed price or selected option initiates a transaction.

Money uses nonnegative integer minor units and explicit currency, with formatting based on the currency's decimal exponent. Study currency support is intentionally bounded to USD/EUR/GBP/JPY/KWD, including zero- and three-decimal tests. Sale comparison requires a larger amount in the same currency and billing basis. Fixed, starting and monthly/yearly subscription amounts remain product-associated. This is not a Pricing/Plans collection.

Product media is optional in the core record, allowing digital goods and unavailable images. Individual storytelling/gallery structures require their own media records. Media union: still, native controlled video, or truthful interactive-placeholder. Product options are 1–3 groups; variants must reference one valid value from every group, with unique combinations and matching currency. No matching record differs from a supplied unavailable variant. Destination labels and host-owned paths are data; Lab clicks open local focus-managed summaries.

## Product-item vocabulary

The shared ProductRecord handles only identity, price, availability and destination semantics. It is not a universal ProductCard. Separate structures are: editorial lead/support item (P01), compact catalog row (P02), numbered look legend with selected record (P06), narrative colophon (P07), comparison heading (P13), and relation-led companion (P14). A contained image versus a missing image, fixed versus sale price, and alternate type/art tokens are configuration/data states, not new components. Minimal tiles and image-dominant fashion cards remain candidates for later production vocabulary; this round does not pad its count with cosmetic card variants.

## Breadth and future-page awareness

The three exercised contexts cover apparel/streetwear/creator merchandise, beauty/cosmetics, hardware and a digital subscription product. The mechanisms could later support luxury jewelry (P03/P08), furniture/home goods (P05/P10/P13), specialty food (P01/P09/P14), automotive products (P02/P08/P13) and boutique retail (P01/P06). Those are proposed mappings, not extra tested industry adaptations. Future page roles are Shop, Collection, Category, Product Detail, Lookbook, Campaign and Search Results. No route convention or fixed template is imposed.

## Design Engine limitations uncovered

1. Production registry categories/page types and SectionContract usage do not yet express commerce or Product Detail blocks. Local study proposal metadata records these without silently widening runtime capabilities.
2. The engine has no normalized commerce adapter or source-of-truth availability. Study schemas are an initial vocabulary, not a claim of provider interoperability. Market pricing, localization, currency range, tax copy and option dependencies need a dedicated approved contract pass.
3. No production 360/3D, zoom, product lightbox, selected-image crossfade or variant-media mapping contract exists. These remain explicit proposals/fallbacks. Existing reveal and horizontal-scroll primitives are suitable future starting points; no Motion expansion was performed.
4. Finite catalog slices are not large-catalog infrastructure. Real search, pagination, count accuracy, facets, filtering URLs and cache freshness remain host responsibilities.
5. Generated imagery makes the relationships reviewable but cannot validate true material, formula, sustainability, fit or supply-chain claims. Images preserve reference intent, not measured SKU accuracy. Production needs client-owned verified photography.
6. No sticky product information is introduced. Flow metadata correctly declares no sticky ownership; future PDP layout must decide that once rather than several blocks competing.
7. Automated accessibility checks and keyboard exercises are review evidence, not a screen-reader certification or a substitute for production testing in Safari, Firefox and real touch devices.
