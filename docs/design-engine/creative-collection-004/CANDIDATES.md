# Collection 004 — experimental reusable shortlist

30 September 2026. S01, S03 and S06 were chosen because they test three different portability problems: media-led evidence, attributed voices and relational decision content. All three remain **experimental 0.1.0**. None has production evidence or human creative approval. All eight original studies remain in the separate Collection 004 gallery.

`composition/schemas.ts` owns executable strict schemas and text/count bounds. Unknown keys, arbitrary CSS props, undeclared media and unsupported motion are rejected. `composition/catalog.ts` owns structures, art/type sets, override permissions, semantic order and accessibility constraints. `registry/story-sections.ts` derives Design registration from those contracts. `sections/storytelling/` holds three independent implementations and token-driven structural CSS. The preview-only `collection-004/candidates.ts` supplies all brand copy, generated media references and content extremes.

Site owns semantic brand tokens and icons. Typography, art direction and motion resolve site → page → permitted section override. Structure, content and media treatment belong to the section. Global motion can remain expressive for another section while these candidates remain invariant at none. Design inspection starts each candidate with its study's type/art defaults; a composition inherits its own site/page context unless a local override is explicit.

All implementations have h2 section headings, subordinate h3s, stable section IDs and a consistent DOM reading order. S01 reuses TreatedImage, captions and focal treatment. S03 requires attribution instead of pretending invented fixture voices are testimony. S06 uses native disclosure: Tab reaches uniquely named summaries, Enter/Space toggle them, focus is visible, and no animation is needed. Column labels remain available to screen readers on desktop and become visible on mobile. Switching structure remounts the disclosure state; the inspector announces that behavior.

## Content extremes and boundaries

Each candidate exposes three unrelated briefs and short/standard/long content in Design. Long fixtures approach schema boundaries (178-character title, 590-character introduction/record body, 890-character answer, 690-character rationale); they test wrapping and flow rather than ellipsis. Mobile changes spatial arrangement without deleting content.

Remaining limitations before any advancement:

- **S01:** intentionally requires exactly three observations and a meaningful source object. No multi-object archive, hotspot system, video or provenance database. Alternative crops require a human focal/alt/credit check.
- **S03:** two–four exchanges; no audio playback, transcript synchronization or consent workflow. Real attributed words must replace invented dialogue, with client approval.
- **S06:** two–five decisions and three fixed comparison dimensions. No scoring, sortable table, filtering or animated accordion. The promises and tradeoffs need editorial approval; disclosure can hide optional rationale but never the main decision.
- **Shared:** English fixture QA in Chrome at three client widths is not exhaustive assistive-technology, browser, localization, licensed-font or client-content certification. Client media rights and font bindings remain project responsibilities. Human creative selection and the existing review/production lifecycle gates are still required.
